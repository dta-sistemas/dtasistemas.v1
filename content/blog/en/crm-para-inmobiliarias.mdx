Most real estate agencies looking for a CRM already tried one before and stopped using it. Not because the CRM was bad, but because it didn't fit how the team actually works. Before choosing (or building) one, it's worth separating what a real estate CRM truly needs from what only adds complexity.

## What you do need

**Automatic lead capture.** If your prospects come from real estate portals, social media, and your own site, the CRM should capture them automatically in one place — not depend on someone manually copying them from email.

**Stage-based follow-up, not just a contact list.** A brand-new lead needs different follow-up than one who already visited a property. The system should reflect what stage each prospect is at and remind you of the next step.

**Property records linked to the prospect.** Knowing which properties interest each client, not just their contact info, is what actually helps close deals.

**Visibility for the sales team, not just each agent.** If everyone keeps their own spreadsheet, leadership can't know what's working and what isn't. This is one of the most common reasons real estate agencies look for a [custom system](/en/soluciones/sistemas-empresariales) instead of a generic CRM.

## What you probably don't need (yet)

- **Complex marketing automations** if your monthly lead volume is low — they add configuration without proportional results.
- **Built-in invoicing or accounting modules**, if you already have an accounting system that works — it's simpler to integrate them than to replace one with the other.
- **"All-in-one" features** that promise to solve marketing, sales, after-sales and admin in a single system — in practice, they tend to do everything mediocrely.

## Generic CRM vs. custom CRM

A generic (off-the-shelf) CRM makes sense when your agency's sales process is similar to any other's. But if you work with pre-sales, your own developments, or a particular decision cycle (for example, sales to foreigners or investors), a custom system usually ends up costing less in the medium term than forcing a generic CRM to fit your real process, piling on plugins and workarounds.
