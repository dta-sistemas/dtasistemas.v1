Almost every company already has data: in the sales system, in the CRM, in the booking platform, in scattered spreadsheets. The problem is almost never a lack of data — it's that nobody has time to turn it into something that helps decide.

## Data is not the same as information

Having a table with ten thousand rows of transactions is having data. Knowing that sales dropped 12% at the north branch over the last two months, and that it coincides with a key salesperson leaving, is information. The difference is the work of turning one into the other — and that work is exactly why most companies end up with only data.

## Why the weekly Excel report doesn't scale

The most common pattern we see: someone on the team builds a report every week or month, copying and pasting from different systems. It works at first, but has three problems that get worse over time:

1. **It depends on one person.** If that person is on vacation or leaves the company, the report doesn't happen.
2. **It arrives late.** By the time the report is ready, the data is already days old.
3. **It doesn't scale.** Adding a new data source means more manual hours, not a system adjustment.

## What a good executive dashboard looks like

A dashboard that leadership actually keeps using (not just opens the first week) usually has three traits:

- **It shows a few KPIs, not every possible data point.** A dashboard with 40 charts doesn't get read — one with 5-8 well-chosen metrics does.
- **It updates itself.** If someone has to "run the report" manually, sooner or later they'll stop doing it.
- **It has alerts, not just charts.** Knowing something went out of range is more useful than having to check the dashboard every day to notice.

This is exactly what we work on in our [Intelligence / Business Intelligence](/en/soluciones/business-intelligence) solution: defining the right KPIs together with leadership, connecting real data sources, and automating updates.

## Starting without building everything at once

You don't need to consolidate all the company's information to start seeing value. One well-built dashboard, on the process leadership cares about most today, is usually a better starting point than a "business intelligence" project that tries to cover everything from day one.

If you're not sure which data is worth consolidating first, a [technology diagnosis](/en/soluciones/dta-digital-audit) helps identify where the information that would have the most impact if visible in real time is hiding.
